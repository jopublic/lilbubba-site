How to Find and Delete Duplicate Videos, Photos and Audio on Windows (Without Losing Anything)

By LilBuba.ai · 28 July 2026 · 6 min read

If you shoot, edit or hoard media, you know the drive-filling horror story. It isn't one big file — it's the same thing, over and over, in slightly different clothes.

The mess creators actually make

  • Final_v3_FINAL.mp4 — times five hundred. Every export, every re-render, every "just one more tweak" leaves another near-identical copy behind.
  • Raw dailies and camera dumps — the same clip ingested twice, or copied "to be safe" onto three drives.
  • Photo bursts — forty near-identical frames of the same moment, plus the RAW and the JPEG of each.
  • Duplicate exports and proxies — the same timeline rendered at different settings, scattered across project folders.

None of it is junk exactly — it's just duplication, and on 4K footage it eats terabytes fast.

Why "just grab a free tool" doesn't cut it here

There are free and open-source duplicate finders out there, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. But they're built to do one thing: find matching files and delete them. For a screenshot you can re-take, that's fine.

For footage you shot once — the wedding, the client edit, the gig you'll never film again — it's a different story. Most tools:

  • only catch byte-for-byte identical files, so they sail straight past the near-duplicates that are your actual problem (the same clip in a different format, the RAW-and-JPEG pair, the burst of near-identical frames);
  • delete outright, with no real safety net — one wrong click on irreplaceable footage and it's gone;
  • choke on OneDrive cloud-only files, either crashing mid-scan or force-downloading your whole library;
  • and they treat your priceless footage exactly like any other disposable file, because they weren't built for creators.
